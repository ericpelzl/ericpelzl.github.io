Welcome to the Puzzl-e Lab! We are Prof. Eric Pelzl and a team of graduate and undergraduate students from PolyU who use behavioral and neuro-imaging (EEG) methods to investigate bilingual speech perception and second language learning. Below is a list of the current lines of research being pursued by members of our lab.

Accented Chinese Speech

Second language speakers often have a noticeable accent. In this project, we try to understand how listeners respond to accented speech and whether they can adapt to different types of accent (regional vs. foreign).

Reports & Publications

  • In preparation Pelzl, E. & Cui, Z. (in prep) An ERP investigation of adaptation to bad maps (mispronunciation) in L2 accented speech
  • Conference presentation Cui, Z. & Pelzl, E. (2025) Native listeners tolerate tonal pronunciation errors (bad maps) in Cantonese-accented Mandarin. Poster presented at 3rd International Conference on Tone and Intonation, Herrsching, Germany.
  • Publication Pelzl, E., Carlson, M. T., Guo, T., Jackson, C. N., & van Hell, J. G. (2021) Tuning out tone errors? Native listeners do not down-weight tones when hearing unsystematic tone errors in foreign-accented Mandarin. Bilingualism: Language and Cognition. 24(1), 215-222. [link]

Learning Chinese Characters

Unlike learning an alphabet, which usually takes days, learning Chinese characters usually takes years. This project investigates whether using different typefaces (fonts) might help in a small way to the task of learning characters.

Reports & Publications

  • In preparation Pelzl, E., Investigating the effects of variable typeface training with naïve learners of Chinese characters: improved generalization comes at a cost
  • Publication Pelzl, E., (2025). High variability orthographic training: Learning words in a logographic script through training with multiple typefaces. Psychonomic Bulletin and Review [link]

Learning Tones in a Second Language

This line of work examines how a person's language experience helps or hinders them in learning the tones of Mandarin. In the future, we hope to develop more effective ways to help people learn tones.

Selected Publications

  • Publication Pelzl, E., Liu, J., & Qi, C. (2022) Native language experience with tones influences both phonetic and lexical processes when acquiring a second tonal language. Journal of Phonetics. 95, 101197. [link]
  • Publication Pelzl, E., Lau, E., Guo, T., & DeKeyser, R., C. (2021a) Even in the best-case scenario L2 learners have persistent difficulty perceiving and utilizing tones in Mandarin: Findings from behavioral and ERP experiments. Studies in Second Language Acquisition. 43(2), 268-296. [link]
  • Publication Pelzl, E. (2019). What makes perception of Mandarin tones difficult for second language learners: A non-technical review of evidence from psycholinguistic research. Chinese as a Second Language. 54(1), 51-78. [link]
